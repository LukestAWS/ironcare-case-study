What We Found
Patient records were open to the internet.

IronCare had been storing patient records in AWS for 18 months. Nobody had checked whether it was configured correctly. Our baseline Prowler scan across 572 security checks found:

Prowler Baseline — 9 March 2026
572 checks · eu-west-2 · Account 470******620 ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ ✗ CRITICAL 2 Root MFA off · Public S3 bucket ✗ HIGH 15 No CloudTrail · No GuardDuty · No backup ✗ MEDIUM 46 No alarms · No encryption policies ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 35% passing · 80 failures across 572 checks
⚠ In plain English
Patient records were accessible to anyone with the bucket address. No alarm, no audit trail, no backup. If records were deleted or stolen — nobody would have known.

Macie also flagged 3 NHS numbers in a publicly accessible bucket — confirming real patient data exposure.
